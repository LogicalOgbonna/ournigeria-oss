### Banks Lending to Government

#### The ISPO Mechanism

Banks lend to state governments secured by an **Irrevocable Standing Payment Order (ISPO)** issued to the Accountant-General of the Federation. This allows monthly deductions directly from the state's FAAC statutory allocation as a first-line charge. Essentially risk-free lending — repayment is guaranteed through automatic revenue deduction before the state receives its allocation.

**This is why FAAC data is critical for banks** — understanding a state's monthly FAAC allocation, existing deductions, and net allocation directly determines lending capacity and risk.

### Credit Rating Agencies

Nigeria has 3 SEC-approved credit rating agencies: **Agusto & Co** (est. 1992), **DataPro Limited** (est. 1995), **Global Credit Rating**.

**What they need for subnational government ratings:**
1. Revenue data (FAAC allocations, IGR breakdown, 3-5 year trends)
2. Expenditure data (recurrent vs capital, personnel costs, debt service)
3. Debt profile (outstanding debt, debt-to-revenue ratio, existing ISPOs)
4. Budget performance (approved vs actual, variance analysis)
5. Governance indicators (financial management capacity, audit reports)
6. Economic indicators (state GDP, diversification, employment)

**Current problem:** Data is obtained manually, inconsistently published by states, often outdated.

**Structured government financial data APIs would be transformative** for these agencies — enabling real-time fiscal assessments instead of manual data collection.

### How MFBs Access Government Data

Microfinance Banks (MFBs) have **very limited structured access** to government financial data. Key facts:
- Industry: total assets N2.8T (June 2024), up 91.3% YoY
- MFBs doing payroll lending rely on manually obtained government payroll data
- National Association of Microfinance Banks (NAMB) doesn't provide centralized government data access
- **Timi's advice validated:** MFBs are easier to sell to (less bureaucracy, clear pain point, smaller deal sizes but faster close)

---

### Regulatory Landscape

**CBN:** Licenses credit bureaus, mandates loan reporting to 2+ bureaus, issued Credit Bureau Act 2017

**DMO (Debt Management Office):** Sets subnational borrowing guidelines — states must specify borrowing purpose, obtain State Executive Council approval, secure House of Assembly resolution, work within debt sustainability thresholds

**Key regulatory gap:** No framework mandating structured, API-accessible government financial data for credit assessment. Current system relies on manual collection from states, NBS publications, DMO reports, and individual state budget documents.

**Opportunity:** OurNigeria fills this gap by providing structured, queryable government financial data that regulators, credit agencies, and banks currently lack.

## Key Takeaways

1. **The civic data market is validated** — BudgIT ($5.8M+ in grants, 101 staff), Dataphyte (multiple grant funders), and Stears ($3.9M raised) prove demand exists for structured Nigerian government data

2. **The gap is clear** — No one provides LGA-level FAAC data, structured state budget data, and corruption case tracking in a single queryable platform with AI access

3. **B2B revenue path is real** — Credit bureaus, credit rating agencies, and banks lending to government have acute, unfilled needs for structured fiscal data. The ISPO mechanism creates direct demand for FAAC allocation data

4. **Start with warm intros** — TechPoint (Timi's contact), TechCabal (civic vertical), IntelPoint (pays for research). These are fastest to close

5. **Lead with partner value** — per Timi: "How YOU make money from this." For credit bureaus: new product line (government credit reports). For publications: data-powered content that drives traffic. For banks: better loan pricing

6. **MFBs are quick wins** — less bureaucracy, clear pain point (manually obtaining government payroll data), smaller deal sizes but faster close. Start here for financial vertical, work up to banks

7. **Stears' pivot left a gap** — consumer-facing data journalism market is underserved. OurNigeria's chatbot + B2C content fills this while B2B revenue sustains operations

8. **MacArthur Foundation is the key funder** — has committed $19.2M to Nigerian investigative journalism/media. Funded Premium Times, TheCable, Daily Trust, BudgIT, Sahara Reporters. OurNigeria fits their mandate
