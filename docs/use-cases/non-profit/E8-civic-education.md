# E8: Secondary School Civic Education Module

**Entity:** OurNigeria Foundation (Non-Profit)
**Priority:** Phase 3
**Timeline:** Month 12+
**Impact potential:** Very High (generational civic culture change)

---

## Problem

Civic education in Nigerian secondary schools is entirely theoretical. Students learn about "government," "democracy," and "public finance" from textbooks without ever interacting with real government data. They can define "budget" and "appropriation" on an exam but cannot tell you:
- How much their state received from FAAC last month
- What percentage of their state's budget goes to education (their own sector!)
- Whether their state's governor is spending more or less than the previous administration

This produces a generation of citizens who understand governance concepts but have never practiced civic engagement with real data. The gap between "knowing about" budgets and "using" budget data is where accountability dies.

---

## Solution

A **simplified, guided interface** for secondary school students to explore their state's fiscal data, paired with lesson plans and teacher guides.

### Student Interface

A simplified version of the OurNigeria chat, designed for SS1-SS3 students (ages 14-17):

- **Guided prompts:** "Ask about your state's budget" instead of an open text box
- **Pre-built explorations:** "How does your state compare to others?" → interactive guided tour
- **Visual-first:** Charts and infographics, minimal dense text
- **Impact framing:** Every number translated to student-relevant equivalents ("N20M = 40 full secondary school scholarships")
- **Quizzes:** After each exploration, simple comprehension questions

### Teacher Resources

- **Lesson plans:** 6-8 lesson modules aligned with NERDC Civic Education curriculum
- **Discussion guides:** "What did you learn about your state? What surprised you?"
- **Assessment templates:** Project-based: "Research your state's health budget and present findings"
- **Teacher dashboard:** Track class engagement, popular queries, learning progress

### Example Lesson Module

```
MODULE 3: "WHERE DOES THE MONEY GO?"
Grade: SS2 | Duration: 2 periods (80 minutes)

Objective: Students will explore their state's budget allocation
by sector and evaluate government spending priorities.

Activity 1 (20 min): Guided Exploration
- Students open OurNigeria student portal
- Follow guided prompt: "Show me [my state]'s budget breakdown"
- Record: Top 3 sectors by spending, % spent on education

Activity 2 (20 min): Comparison
- Students compare their state with 2 neighboring states
- Discuss: Why might spending priorities differ?

Activity 3 (20 min): Impact Translation
- Use impact calculator: "What could your state's education
  budget buy?"
- Calculate: Schools, teachers, textbooks, scholarships

Activity 4 (20 min): Reflection
- Class discussion: "Is your state spending enough on education?"
- Written reflection: "If I were governor, I would change..."
- Homework: Share one finding with a family member
```

## Technical Requirements

1. **Simplified student interface** — Guided prompts, visual-first, age-appropriate language (3-4 weeks)
2. **Lesson plan content** — 6-8 modules with teacher guides (2-3 weeks, content creation)
3. **Quiz/assessment engine** — Simple comprehension questions after explorations (1-2 weeks)
4. **Teacher dashboard** — Class management, engagement tracking (2-3 weeks)
5. **Offline mode** — Many Nigerian schools have intermittent internet; cache key data (1-2 weeks)

**Total estimated effort:** 10-14 weeks

---

## Rollout Strategy

### Phase 1: Pilot (Month 12-14)
- Partner with 5 schools across 3 states (urban, semi-urban, rural mix)
- Train 10-15 teachers
- Test 3 lesson modules
- Collect student and teacher feedback

### Phase 2: State Pilot (Month 14-18)
- Expand to 50 schools in 3 states
- Full 8-module curriculum
- State Ministry of Education endorsement
- Teacher training workshops

### Phase 3: National Scale (Month 18+)
- NERDC curriculum integration
- 500+ schools across 15+ states
- Digital and printed materials
- Annual "Civic Data Day" competition

---

## Impact Metrics

- **Schools reached:** Number of schools using the module
- **Students engaged:** Active users of the student portal
- **Civic knowledge:** Pre/post assessment scores on fiscal literacy
- **Family spillover:** Students sharing findings with family members (survey)
- **Long-term tracking:** Are these students more civically engaged as young adults?

---

## Why Phase 3 (Not Sooner)

1. Education partnerships require institutional trust — needs established OurNigeria brand
2. NERDC curriculum approval takes 6-12 months
3. Teacher training infrastructure needs funding (grants)
4. Content must be pedagogically sound (hire education consultants)
5. Core platform must be stable and battle-tested before student use
