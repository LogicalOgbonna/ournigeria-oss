# E6: Health Spending Accountability Tracker (Abuja Declaration)

**Entity:** OurNigeria Foundation (Non-Profit)
**Priority:** Grant-driven (part of unified Sector Accountability Dashboard)
**Timeline:** Month 6+
**Impact potential:** High (largest global health funder pool)

---

## Strategic Decision

E6 shares implementation with E4 (Gender) and E5 (Climate) — one Sector Accountability Dashboard, three grant pitches.

---

## Problem

In 2001, African heads of state signed the **Abuja Declaration**, committing to allocate at least **15% of national budgets to health.** Twenty-five years later, Nigeria has never met this target. Most states allocate 4-8% to health.

Health outcomes reflect this underinvestment:
- Maternal mortality: 512 per 100,000 live births (one of the highest globally)
- Under-5 mortality: 117 per 1,000 (among the worst in Africa)
- Doctor-to-patient ratio: 1:5,000+ (WHO recommends 1:1,000)
- 60% of healthcare spending is out-of-pocket

The global health community (WHO, Global Fund, GAVI, PEPFAR, Gates Foundation) invests billions in Nigerian health but lacks structured data on:
- Which states allocate the most to health?
- Which states actually spend their health budget? (Execution rate)
- Is health spending increasing or declining by state?
- How does spending break down by sub-sector (primary care, hospitals, immunization, maternal health)?
- What's the relationship between health spending and health outcomes?

---

## Solution

A **Health Budget Accountability Dashboard** — the clearest use case for the Sector Accountability Dashboard because the benchmark (15%) is universally agreed.

### Core Metrics

| Metric | What It Measures | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| **Health budget share** | Health allocation as % of total budget | 15% (Abuja Declaration) |
| **Health execution rate** | Health budget spent vs allocated | 80%+ is good |
| **Per-capita health spending** | Health budget / state population | WHO: $86/person minimum |
| **Primary health care share** | PHC allocation vs total health | 60%+ of health budget |
| **Capital vs recurrent** | Investment in new health infrastructure vs salaries | 30%+ capital is healthy |
| **Health payment discipline** | GovSpend payments to health contractors/suppliers | From A1 scores |
| **Trend (5-year)** | Direction of all above metrics | Improving or declining? |

### Dashboard Views

1. **Abuja Declaration Scorecard** — All 37 states against the 15% target
2. **Health spending per capita map** — Geographic heat map showing health spending inequality
3. **Execution gap analysis** — Allocated vs spent, with drill-down by sub-sector
4. **State comparison** — Side-by-side comparison of any 2-5 states
5. **Trend tracker** — 5-year trajectories for each metric
6. **What the gap costs** — "If Kano allocated 15% to health, it could build X more clinics, hire Y more doctors"

## Grant Application Narrative

> "Twenty-five years after the Abuja Declaration, only 2 of Nigeria's 37 states allocate the promised 15% of their budgets to health. The rest allocate 4-8% — while 70,000 women die in childbirth each year and 117 of every 1,000 children don't survive to age 5. The OurNigeria Health Spending Tracker makes these numbers visible for the first time: a state-by-state dashboard showing health budget allocations, execution rates, per-capita spending, and the human cost of the gap. When communities can see that their state spends N780 per person on health — compared to N8,200 in Lagos — they can demand change with evidence, not just anger."
