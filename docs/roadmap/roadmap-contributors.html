OurNigeria Roadmap · contributors · 2026

Open source · open gaps

Eight things left to build. Five engines to build them on.

The geography is done — 37 states, 774 local governments, 8,807 wards, 1,468 constituencies, 2,317 elective seats, all resolvable from a location. What is left is not eight separate products. It is five engines, three of which already exist and need extending rather than inventing. This page is the map of what is missing and where a contributor actually plugs in.

If you are looking for the shortest useful contribution: two in every three tracked officials are a name and nothing else. That gap needs no code at all.
01

Five engines, not eight features

The eight roadmap items collapse hard once you look at their shape. Three of them are the same registry problem; three more are the same places problem. Building them as one engine each is the difference between a year of work and three.

ENGINE 01

Registry — a person, holding a role, in a jurisdiction, for a term

Exists · extend it

This already runs the whole elected-officials surface. A person record, a position record binding them to a scope, a term, a party, and a full provenance trail — source, source URL, confidence, last verified.

The important thing for contributors: the police hierarchy and the ministries layer are the same shape. A Commissioner of Police is a person holding a role scoped to a state. A DPO is a person holding a role scoped to a division. A minister is a person holding a role scoped to a ministry. None of that needs a new engine — it needs new roles, a new scope type, and the data to fill it.

  • PowersElected officials · police command · ministries
  • Live now2,847 officials across 37 states
  • MissingAppointed security roles, ministry leadership, division-level scope

ENGINE 02

Places — the hierarchy, and what physically sits inside it

Exists to ward · needs a floor below

State, local government and ward are all live and addressable. The ward is currently the floor, and that is the blocker for three roadmap items at once.

Communities, public hospitals and public schools are one engine wearing three hats: a settlement layer beneath the ward, then facilities attached to settlements and rolled up to the LGA. Communities must land first — the other two are cheap once it exists, and impossible before.

Note for anyone reaching for maps: there is no geometry in the database today. Boundaries are GeoJSON files loaded at runtime, state and LGA only, and ward-level reverse geocoding is not implemented.

  • PowersCommunities · health facilities · schools
  • Live now774 LGAs, 8,807 wards, addressable by slug
  • MissingCommunity layer, facility records, ward geometry

ENGINE 03

Elections — contests, parties, candidates, slates

Built · needs filling

This engine exists and runs. There is a public party directory and a profile page per party, a ballot API that resolves races from a location, and a working personaliser that fetches senate, reps, state assembly, chairman and councillor races for a given ward.

What it lacks is candidates and a front door. Seven candidates are on record for the 2027 presidential race and none for governor in any state sampled. There is no Election or Candidate table — a candidate is an official_elections row marked as a won primary. The whole surface sits behind a feature flag that fails closed, is reachable only through a geo rewrite, and the state page is deliberately de-indexed.

  • PowersParty directory · party profiles · personalised ballot
  • Live now16 parties, ballot API, 2,317 seats to attach to
  • MissingCandidate coverage, the public year-scoped routes, a switched-on front door

ENGINE 04

Legislation — bills, sponsors, hearings, local impact

New

Nothing exists here yet. A bill needs a record, a sponsor and backers linked to existing official profiles, a status history, and — the interesting part — a link to the places it actually affects, so it can surface on the right LGA pages.

National Assembly hearings stream publicly. Pulling transcripts, summarising them, and attaching them to the right bill is a self-contained piece of work that touches almost nothing else in the codebase, which makes it unusually good for a contributor working alone.

  • PowersBill tracker · hearing summaries · follow-a-bill alerts
  • Live nowOfficial profiles to link sponsors to
  • MissingEverything — records, transcript pipeline, impact routing, subscriptions

ENGINE 05

Money — allocation traced federal to local

Live · deepening

The oldest engine in the project and the most built-out. Hundreds of budget documents and hundreds of thousands of payment records are ingested, embedded and queryable in plain English or Pidgin.

The remaining work is depth rather than existence: tracing an allocation cleanly from the federal purse through the state to a specific local government, and linking budgeted capital projects to the places they were supposed to be built.

  • PowersBudget search · allocations · spending records · project tracking
  • Live now700+ documents, 891K+ payment records
  • MissingClean federal → state → LGA trace; project-to-place links

03

Three ways in

Without writing code

The largest gap on the whole roadmap is filled this way, and today it means exactly one thing: identifying public officials. Submit a photograph, a phone number, an office address for an official in your area.

Submissions are voted on and moderated before anything goes live. This is already open at /proposals/new.

Next to open: identifying and mapping infrastructure — stations, clinics, schools, and the communities they serve. Same submit-and-vote flow, but it needs the places engine underneath it first.

Verifying what is already there

Every record carries a source and a confidence level. Plenty are marked low, or were verified long enough ago to be stale — officials die, get sacked, cross-carpet, and the database does not find out on its own.

Checking a claim against a real source and flagging what is wrong is worth more than adding new unverified rows.

Writing code

  • Frontend: Next.js and React — the public civic app and its state, LGA and ward pages.
  • Backend: NestJS and Prisma over PostgreSQL — the geography, registry and proposal APIs.
  • Pipelines: document and structured-data ingestion, the area with the most self-contained work available.
04

Ground rules, because this data is about real people

Provenance is not optional

Every claim about a person carries a source, a source URL, a date and a confidence level. A contribution without a source is not a contribution — it is a rumour with better formatting.

Records, not accusations

We publish what documents say and link to them. We do not editorialise about individuals, and the legal register stays strictly read-only. This is the difference between accountability work and a defamation suit.

Public provision only

Hospitals and schools mean public hospitals and public schools. Private provision is a market and is somebody else’s subject. What is being audited here is what the state promised its citizens for free.
